Module 1

Organisational Context & Approach (OA)

Syllabus Area 1

1.1 Drivers of Change: External vs. Internal Context

Organizations do not exist in a vacuum; they operate inside a continuous flow of environmental and internal pressures that compel transformation:

🌍 External Drivers (PESTLE)

Factors beyond the organization's direct control. Includes Political (legislation, trade treaties), Economic (inflation, recessions), Societal (demographic changes, hybrid work expectations), Technological (AI, cloud automation), and Legal/Environmental mandates.

🏢 Internal Drivers

Pressures emerging from within: new corporate strategy, leadership transitions, operational performance gaps, culture clashes, change maturity level, and historical legacy of past changes.

⚠️ The Critical Impact of "History of Change": When organizations suffer from a legacy of failed, abandoned, or painful past changes, employees develop change fatigue, cynicism, and reflexive resistance. Conversely, organizations with transparent, well-delivered past changes have built psychological capital and trust.

1.2 The Cynefin Framework (Dave Snowden)

The Cynefin framework helps change leaders diagnose the nature of the operating environment so they don't apply rigid, bureaucratic recipes to emergent, unpredictable problems:

1. Simple / Obvious Domain (Best Practice)

Characteristics: Stable cause-and-effect relationship known to everyone. Predictable, repeatable.
Decision Flow: Sense → Categorise → Respond.
🏢 Real-World Example: Transitioning office printer supplies or rolling out standard HR expense reimbursement guidelines.

2. Complicated Domain (Good Practice)

Characteristics: Cause-and-effect is discoverable, but requires specialized expertise, technical diagnosis, and deep analysis.
Decision Flow: Sense → Analyse → Respond.
🏢 Real-World Example: Migrating legacy on-premise relational databases into an enterprise cloud architecture (AWS/Azure) requiring data architects.

3. Complex Domain (Emergent Practice)

Characteristics: Cause-and-effect can only be perceived in hindsight. The right questions are unknown. High interconnected human dynamics.
Decision Flow: Probe → Sense → Respond. Safe-to-fail experiments, rapid feedback loops, and co-design.
🏢 Real-World Example: Attempting to transform a 50-year-old siloed bank hierarchy into a customer-centric, cross-functional agile innovation culture.

4. Chaotic Domain (Novel Practice)

Characteristics: Immediate crisis; turbulence; no clear cause-and-effect patterns exist.
Decision Flow: Act → Sense → Respond. Take immediate directive command to stop the bleeding, restore order, and shift into Complex.
🏢 Real-World Example: Immediate emergency response to a massive corporate ransomware attack locking all hospital surgical operations.

1.3 Planned vs. Emergent Change & Kelman's Adoption

Dimension Planned Change Emergent Change
Starting Point & Goals Clear beginning, defined intermediate milestones, fixed endpoint. Direction is clear, but exact destination evolves iteratively.
Management Style Programmed, structured, sequential control (Waterfall-like). Experimental, participatory co-design, learning-by-doing.
Best Suited For Office relocations, regulatory compliance, ERP migrations. Culture transformation, continuous service improvement, innovation.
Kelman’s Three Levels of Adoption:
  • • 1. Compliance (Surface Level): Staff adopt new behaviours solely to secure incentives or avoid disciplinary action. Reverts immediately when supervision disappears.
  • • 2. Identification (Relational Level): Staff adopt new practices to maintain positive rapport with or belong to a respected leader or team.
  • • 3. Internalization (Deep Level): The change resonates directly with the individual's core beliefs and values. The behaviour becomes permanent, autonomous, and self-sustaining.
🎯 Exam Rule: Ownership of Benefits

Business Leadership is ultimately accountable for realizing business benefits. The Change Manager does not own the benefits; instead, the Change Manager facilitates the human readiness, engagement architectures, and adoption environments that enable business leaders to harvest those benefits.

Module 2

People and Change (PC)

Syllabus Area 2

2.1 Conscious Competence Learning Curve & The "Learning Dip"

Whenever individuals are asked to adopt a new system, tool, or behavior, their performance does not improve linearly. Noel Burch’s model explains why performance inevitably plunges before it ascends:

Stage 1: Unconscious Incompetence ("Blissful Ignorance")

The user does not know what they do not know. They believe the change is easy because they have not yet touched the operational complexity.

Stage 2: Conscious Incompetence (THE LEARNING DIP 📉)

The user begins working in the new reality and suddenly experiences friction: they cannot find function buttons, tasks take three times longer, errors spike, and confidence evaporates.
🏢 Real-World Example: A customer service rep who used to handle 20 calls/hour on an old mainframe is switched to a modern CRM and can only handle 6 calls/hour because they are hunting for fields.
Management Imperative: Provide patience, peer coaches, psychological safety, and realistic transition productivity targets.

Stage 3: Conscious Competence ("Concentrated Effort")

The user can perform the task successfully, but every step requires deliberate, conscious cognitive focus and checklist verification.

Stage 4: Unconscious Competence ("Second Nature Mastery")

Execution is fluid, intuitive, and automatic. Cognitive energy is freed for creative problem solving.

2.2 Beckhard & Harris Change Formula: D × V × F > R

Dissatisfaction (D) × Vision (V) × First Steps (F) > Resistance (R)

This is a diagnostic formula used to evaluate where to concentrate intervention efforts. Because the left side is a mathematical product:

  • Dissatisfaction (D): People must see why the current state is intolerable (urgency). If $D = 0$, complacency kills change.
  • Vision (V): A clear, tangible future state that people want to inhabit. If $V = 0$, anxiety and confusion reign.
  • First Steps (F): Achievable, actionable starting steps. If $F = 0$, people feel paralyzed by the sheer size of the mountain.
  • The Zero Rule: If ANY factor ($D$, $V$, or $F$) equals 0, the entire left side becomes 0. Resistance ($R$) automatically wins!

2.3 Edgar Schein: Survival Anxiety vs. Learning Anxiety

MIT organizational psychologist Edgar Schein established that change only begins when:

Survival Anxiety > Learning Anxiety
Survival Anxiety (Urgency) The fear that if we do not adapt, the organization will fail, our skills will become obsolete, or our jobs will disappear.
Learning Anxiety (Barrier) The social and personal fear of looking stupid, feeling incompetent during transition, or losing status.
💡 Key Leadership Takeaway: You can create movement either by increasing survival anxiety (threatening people) or by reducing learning anxiety. Schein proved that increasing fear triggers defensive hostility; the only healthy, sustainable path is reducing learning anxiety through Psychological Safety, training, and coaching.

2.4 Kanter's Resistance Drivers & Interventions

Loss of Control / Territory

Solution: EMPOWERMENT. Give them choices, autonomy, and participation in co-designing the local workflow.

Excess Uncertainty

Solution: INFORMATION. Provide consistent, structured, transparent communication updates.

Fear of Incompetence

Solution: TRAINING & SUPPORT. Create safe sandbox environments and dedicated mentorship.

Module 3

Change Leadership & Teams (CT)

Syllabus Area 3

3.1 Role Distinction & Communication Governance

One of the most heavily tested areas in APMG is knowing who owns what message and who holds ultimate accountability:

👑 The Sponsor (Executive Level)

Secures funding, builds coalition at board level, breaks strategic deadlocks. Senders of the high-level strategic "Why" (business drivers, risks of inaction). They DO NOT manage day-to-day team tasks.

👔 Line Leader / Direct Supervisor

Preferred sender of Personal Impact ("What's In It For Me? / WIIFM") messages. They contextualize the change for direct reports and manage daily operational adoption.

🛠️ Change Agent (Local Advocate)

Facilitator and network bridge. Shares feedback between front line and sponsors. CRITICAL RULE: Change agents must NEVER usurp or take over the line leader's operational authority!

3.2 Patrick Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions of a Team

Lencioni visualizes team dysfunction as a pyramid that must be cured from the bottom foundation upward:

5. INATTENTION TO RESULTS → Focus on Collective Team Results (The Apex)
4. AVOIDANCE OF ACCOUNTABILITY → Peer-to-Peer Mutual Accountability
3. LACK OF COMMITMENT → Clarity and Buy-In Behind Decisions
2. FEAR OF CONFLICT → Passionate, Unfiltered Conflict over Ideas
1. ABSENCE OF TRUST → Vulnerability-Based Trust (Foundation)

Exam Focus: "Focus on Team Results" follows on from all other levels. You cannot demand focus on team results if members distrust each other, avoid conflict, and refuse to hold one another accountable.

Module 4

Stakeholders & Communication (SC)

Syllabus Area 4

4.1 Carolyn Taylor: Culture Communication Mechanisms

In Walking the Talk, Carolyn Taylor defines culture as "the patterns of behaviour that are encouraged, discouraged, or tolerated by people and systems over time." Culture is broadcast via 3 mechanisms:

1. Behaviours

Day-to-day interactions: how leaders run meetings, respond to mistakes, treat frontline staff, and role-model values.

2. Symbols (HIGH EXAM VALUE)

Visible signals: how leaders structure and spend their time, physical office layouts, who gets promoted, and budget allocations.

3. Systems

Formal mechanisms: HR compensation policies, KPIs, procurement workflows, and IT operating frameworks.

4.2 Communication Channel Dynamics

• One-Way vs. Two-Way: A poster advertising campaign or company newsletter is one-way, lean, asynchronous broadcast. Two-way verbal dialogue (town halls, 1-on-1s) enables feedback and dynamic clarification.

• Contextualizing for Diverse Audiences: Verbal dialogue is by far the most effective approach for tailoring complex strategic messages to the unique needs of diverse operational teams.

• Narrative and Storytelling: Sharing past experiences and authentic personal stories cuts through corporate jargon and creates emotional resonance.

Module 5

Work of the Change Manager (WM)

Syllabus Area 5

5.1 Change Strategy vs. Business Case

Business Case ("Why Invest?")

Strategic and financial justification. Outlines financial ROI, investment risks, market opportunities, and project deliverables.

Change Strategy ("How to Manage?")

Explains how the human transition will be governed, supported, measured, and sustained. Sets adoption milestones and training approaches.

5.2 Metrics & Delivery Scorecards

When designing a Change Delivery Scorecard, the defining selection criterion is Relevance—ensuring every KPI tracks actionable progress toward strategic goals.

• Quantitative Data: Number-based, countable, measurable (e.g., login counts, ticket volumes, completion % rates). Answers "What and how much?"

• Qualitative Data: Descriptive, narrative feedback (e.g., interview transcripts, focus group notes, sentiment surveys). Answers "Why and how did this happen?"

5.3 Agile Change Frameworks

Minimum Viable Change: Used in Prioritization to select the smallest high-value change increment.
Kanban: Visual work-in-progress (WIP) flow to prevent capacity overload.
Daily Stand-Ups: 15-minute daily operational check-in to clear task blockers.
Retrospectives: Post-sprint reflective learning to continually adjust.
